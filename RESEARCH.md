# Research leads

Where the next branches are most likely to come from, in the order I would chase them.

Everything here is drawn from what the four sheets already say — the gaps, the loose threads
and the two or three places where a single record would open a whole new line. Nothing below
has been verified; each item names the record to look for and where it lives.

## 1. Thomas George Cyril Neil — the wall the tree is named after

**b. 2 November 1913, Plaistow, London · m. Elsie Alexandra Inward 21 Aug 1937 · divorced 1949
· d. 14 March 1970, Guildford**

He is the earliest Neil on any sheet and his parents are not recorded. Everything called
"Neil" or "McNeil" in this tree descends from a man whose own father and mother are unknown.
This is the single highest-value gap.

- **GRO birth index**, Q4 1913 or Q1 1914, West Ham registration district. The GRO index is
  free to search once registered and — unlike FreeBMD — **shows the mother's maiden name**.
  That one field names a second family.
- Then the **1921 Census** (Findmypast) for the household he was seven years old in, and the
  **1939 Register** for him and Elsie as a married couple.
- His 1937 marriage certificate names **his father** outright. GRO marriage index Q3/Q4 1937,
  then order the certificate.

Order of attack: GRO index → 1937 marriage certificate → 1921 census. Expect a new surname
within a couple of evenings.

## 2. Did Edith Lilly Inward really die on the Titanic?

**b. 1885, West Ham · m. Edward Bagley · sheet records her death only as "d. 'TITANIC'"**

She would have been 27 in April 1912. The sheet gives no other detail — no class, no ship's
role, no port. This is either a remarkable family fact or a story that drifted.

- **Encyclopedia Titanica** (encyclopedia-titanica.org) lists every passenger and crew member
  by name, free. Search *Inward* and *Bagley*.
- The **1912 Board of Trade crew agreements** and the Titanic passenger lists are also on
  Findmypast and Ancestry.
- If nothing: check the GRO death index 1912–1920 for Edith Lilly Bagley. A death at sea in
  another year, or another wreck entirely, would explain the note.

Either answer is worth having, and it settles a claim the page currently repeats.

## 3. The Butcher marriages — the join between Tree 1 and Tree 2

This is the structural prize. Two Innard men married two Butcher women in the same small Kent
parish within nine years:

| Sheet | |
|---|---|
| **Tree 1** | Samuel Innard = **Mary Butcher**, m. 1758, Swanscombe. He d. 1791, she d. 1762 |
| **Tree 2** | Jeremiah Innard (Enward) = **Sarah Butcher**, "Swanscombe 1767". He d. 1773 |

Swanscombe was a village of a few hundred people. Two Butcher brides and two Innard grooms in
one parish in one generation is very unlikely to be coincidence — Mary and Sarah are plausibly
sisters, or Samuel and Jeremiah brothers.

- **Swanscombe parish registers** (St Peter & St Paul), held at the **Kent History and Library
  Centre**, Maidstone. Look for Butcher baptisms c. 1730–1750 and Innard/Innard marriages
  1750–1775.
- **FreeREG** (freereg.org.uk) has growing Kent parish coverage, free.
- **FamilySearch** holds microfilmed Kent registers, free.

If it holds, roughly ninety people currently sitting in Tree 2 as a separate cluster join the
main line, and the Inward ancestry pushes back another generation.

## 4. The five unrecorded A'Monge children

David A'Monge and Maureen Theresa Ryan "had ten children in all"; the sheets name five —
David, Bella, Millicent, Mary and Elizabeth Theresa.

**"Aunty Mary Vincent"** is the strongest candidate for a sixth. The summary sheet names her
as Barbara Woolley's godmother, living with her in Calcutta, in the same sentence as Aunts
Millie Collard and Mary Robertson — both of whom *are* A'Monge sisters with married surnames.
Vincent is very likely another married name. She is in the tree unlinked, pending proof.

- **FIBIS** (search.fibis.org) — free, and the specialist database for exactly this.
- **British India Office births and baptisms** via Findmypast — Assam (Nazira, Sivasagar) and
  Calcutta, roughly 1885–1910.
- Calcutta Catholic parish registers; Loretto Convent records, which Barbara attended with her
  Collard cousins.

## 5. Joel Jefferson Buck's honours — free, and near-certain to land

**b. 15 September 1885, Bareilly · Confidential Principal Private Secretary to the Governor of
Bihar, seven Governors, 36 years · Kaisar-i-Hind, Companion of the Imperial Service Order,
AFI Marksman, Coronation Medal · d. 5 October 1960**

Every one of those awards was published in **The London Gazette**, which is free and fully
searchable at [thegazette.co.uk](https://www.thegazette.co.uk). Gazette entries give the exact
date, the exact office held and the spelling of his name as officially recorded.

This is the easiest confirmed record in the whole tree and it dates his career precisely.
Start there before anything paid.

## 6. The India records — Buck, Woolley and A'Monge

The sheets are unusually rich here and the records exist.

- **Herbert Leonard Woolley**, b. 31 Aug 1894, soldier in the British Territorial Army,
  m. 10 Oct 1917 at the Church of the Immaculate Conception, Waverley, Mussoorie. His death is
  not recorded at all. Look for: WWI service and medal rolls; the India Office marriage entry;
  addresses at 4 Hilperton Road, Trowbridge and Suite 21 Samavaya Mansions, Calcutta.
- **Prialodta Buck**, a Khasi princess, no dates at all, buried at **Gresham, Norfolk**. Norfolk
  Record Office holds the Gresham burial registers; a burial entry would give a date and an
  approximate age — the only hard facts she would have. Her marriage to Joel is an India Office
  record.
- **Edward George Buck**, b. 6 Jan 1913 Doranda, Ranchi — India Office births and baptisms.
- **Barbara Woolley**, b. 4 Aug 1923 Calcutta "on arrival" — a shipping passenger list for the
  1923 voyage would name everyone travelling with her, including her mother and the aunts.

## 8. Quick confirmations that tidy the existing tree

- **Elsie Alexandra Inward**, b. 27 July 1914 — GRO birth index should confirm *Major* as her
  mother's maiden name and pin the district.
- **Percy William Inward**, m. 10 September 1892 — GRO marriage index names the parish and
  confirms Jennette Elizabeth Major's father.
- **The dashed branches.** Every link drawn as a dashed line on the page is inferred from where
  a name sits on the sheet, not from a stated relationship. The Jeremiah Inward (b. 1784) →
  Henry Inward (b. 1814) → George Inward (b. 1840) chain carries the whole Inward descent and
  rests on layout alone. Three baptism entries in the Swanscombe or Stone registers would turn
  the main line from inference into fact.

  dated 1972 when she was born in 1981; William Innard b. 1786 dying in 1782; David A'Monge
  "b. 18.7.1816/7" with children born in 1929. Each is a transcription slip on the original
  worth correcting against a certificate.

## Working method

1. **Export the GEDCOM** from the About panel and upload it to FamilySearch (free) or Ancestry.
   Their record matching runs against these 258 people and proposes censuses and certificates
   without you searching for them.
2. **Start with the free indexes** — GRO, FreeBMD, FreeREG, FamilySearch, The London Gazette,
   NSW BDM. Check whether Cardiff or Newport libraries give you free home access to Ancestry
   Library Edition or Findmypast before subscribing.
3. **Buy the certificate only when it will name someone new.** A GRO digital image is £3, a PDF
   £8, a full certificate £12.50. The 1937 marriage certificate in lead 1 is worth every penny;
   most others are not.
4. **Bring findings back to `data.js`** with a real source code, so the page never blurs what
   the sheets said with what you later proved.
